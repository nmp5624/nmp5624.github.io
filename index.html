Finite-Sample Minimax-Regret Treatment Choice with Interval-Observed Outcomes (2026)

Working paper

Abstract

Consider a setup where a decision maker has to choose whether to apply treatment to a population after observing a finite sample. Stoye (2009) characterises the solution to the minimax regret problem in this setup when a finite sample consists of actual outcome realizations. However, in many applied problems, instead of the realized outcomes, the decision maker observes only the intervals containing them. We study a finite-sample minimax regret problem in this setting. With no restriction on interval width, assigning treatment with probability 1/2 yields minimax regret. With widths bounded by h0 and h1 in the two arms, we first show that minimax regret converges to (h0+h1)/4 in the limiting experiment and derive a corresponding asymptotically optimal plug-in treatment rule. Then, for h0+h1≥1, we derive an explicit minimax regret rule which attains this value in finite samples. For h0+h1<1, we establish the existence of an optimal treatment rule and characterize it implicitly through a well-behaved finite-dimensional optimization problem. A simulation experiment then shows that the derived analytic rule produces superior finite-sample minimax regret relative to several heuristic alternatives, with the plug-in rule coming very close when the sample size is sufficiently large.
